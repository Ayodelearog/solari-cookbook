## What Solari makes possible

Solari exposes cloud Chrome sessions through Playwright/CDP, reusable profiles,
session recording, and isolated Linux sandboxes. Sandboxes can run commands,
maintain code state, read and write files, snapshot prepared environments, and
execute multiple independent workers. This supports a traceable retrieval and
document-processing pipeline rather than a browser-only demo.

## Market signals

- Nigeria has an official Open Contracting Portal, but a WTO review notes that
  only part of federal procurement was published there and that the Federal
  Tenders Journal was not available online at the time of its source material.
  This supports the fragmentation thesis but needs current customer validation.
- Real Nigerian notices can require multiple current certificates and evidence
  of several comparable jobs. One official 2026 USPF notice illustrates the
  high cost of missing a single mandatory item.
- Multiple products already sell tender aggregation, alerts, AI matching, and bid
  assistance in Africa and elsewhere. This validates spend but rules out “all
  tenders in one dashboard” as a sufficient differentiator.
- Public competitor pricing ranges from approximately $49/month for monitoring
  to $199/month for pursuit management in examples reviewed. This is directional,
  not a pricing recommendation for Nigeria.

Risk scores are higher when the MVP has fewer security, legal, or platform-policy
hazards. Tender decision support wins because it combines a valuable decision,
a credible local wedge, accessible public inputs, and a strong agent demo.

## Key differentiation

Do not compete on the size of a scraped feed. Begin with one URL and answer one
high-value question exceptionally well. The differentiators are:

- company-specific hard-gate analysis rather than generic summaries;
- evidence on every material claim;
- explicit unknowns and conflicts;
- local compliance vocabulary and document types;
- human correction and an audit trail; and
- honest decision support rather than autonomous submission.

## Riskiest assumptions

1. Nigerian SMEs lose enough time or money on initial tender qualification to pay.
2. They will provide a sufficiently accurate capability profile.
3. Official notices and documents are accessible reliably enough for the MVP.
4. Evidence-linked extraction can reach acceptable hard-gate recall.
5. A narrow decision product is valuable without a built-in discovery feed.
