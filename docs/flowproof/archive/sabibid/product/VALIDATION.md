# Validation plan

The purpose of validation is to earn the right to build more—not to collect
compliments about an attractive demo.

## Week-zero concierge test

Recruit 10 people from these groups:

- small contractors or suppliers who have submitted at least one tender;
- business-development staff at consultancies or service firms; and
- independent bid/procurement consultants who manage several clients.

Ask each participant to bring one real tender considered in the last six months.
Do not begin by pitching features. Reconstruct their last decision:

- How did they discover it?
- Who performed the initial review and how long did it take?
- What made them pursue or abandon it?
- Which requirement was easiest to miss?
- What did a bad pursuit cost in time, fees, or outside help?
- What evidence would they need before trusting a pass recommendation?
- Have they paid anyone or any tool for this work?

Manually produce the proposed SabiBid brief for five real tender packs. Measure
time spent, corrected requirements, missed hard gates, and whether the brief
would have changed the decision.

## Demand tests

Evidence strength, from weakest to strongest:

1. Person says the idea is useful.
2. Person supplies a real tender and company profile.
3. Person returns to analyse a second tender.
4. Person introduces another bidder or consultant.
5. Person agrees to a paid pilot.
6. Person pays.

The first public launch should offer a limited number of free, real analyses in
exchange for permission to publish redacted before/after case studies. Never
publish customer documents or identity without explicit consent.

## Stop or pivot conditions

Reconsider the wedge if, after 10 qualified interviews:

- fewer than three users experienced meaningful qualification pain;
- reviewers do not trust evidence-linked analysis even after corrections;
- documents cannot be retrieved from at least half of chosen sources legally and
  reliably; or
- no participant advances beyond verbal interest.

## Challenge proof

The public challenge demonstration should show a real, reproducible path:

1. Submit an official tender URL.
2. Watch a recorded/redacted Solari retrieval run.
3. Show the downloaded pack processed in a Solari sandbox.
4. Compare requirements against a fictional but realistic company profile.
5. Open citations for hard gates and correct one uncertain extraction.
6. Produce a final pursue/investigate/pass brief.

Publish the architecture, evaluation results, known limitations, and unit cost.
Avoid vanity claims such as “fully autonomous” or “guaranteed compliant.”
